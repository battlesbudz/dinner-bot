Dinner Bot — Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 27, 2026

Dinner Bot is a meal-planning app developed by Justin Battles (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information the app handles and what happens to it. The short version: your recipes, prices, meal plans, and pantry data stay on your device. There is no Dinner Bot account and no Dinner Bot server. The app contains no advertising and no analytics or tracking software.

What stays on your device

Everything you put into Dinner Bot is stored only in the app’s private storage on your device:

  • Your recipes, including recipes you write or import, and any photos you attach to them
  • Your price book: items, prices you set or correct, and the stores you shop at
  • Your meal plans, grocery lists, and pantry and stock records
  • Your settings: household size, weekly budget, the stores you picked, your won’t-eat list, and reminder times
  • Your weekly check-in answers and, if you use Premium, your savings history
  • Counters the app uses for the free plan’s monthly limits (imports and scans), and a cached copy of your Premium status

We cannot see any of this. It is never sent to us. If you delete the app, this data is deleted with it, except for any copies held in Android’s own device backup for your Google account, which you control in your device’s backup settings.

The starter prices the app ships with are national averages. They are reference data built into the app, not information about you.

The few times the app talks to the internet

Barcode lookups (Open Food Facts)

When you scan a barcode and the product is not already in your price book, the app may send the barcode number only to Open Food Facts, a public product database, to suggest the product’s name, pack size, and the nutrition facts printed on its label. The request carries nothing about you or your household beyond the barcode, your IP address, and a user-agent string that names the app. Whatever comes back is shown to you as a suggestion; nothing is saved unless you confirm it. Scanning and linking products works without a connection — the lookup is skipped and everything proceeds offline.

Recipe import from a link

If you paste or share a web link to import a recipe, the app fetches that one page, once, directly from the site you gave it, to read the recipe on it. The site receives the same kind of request it would get from a browser visit. The app sends nothing about you or your household with the request, and the recipe it reads is stored only on your device.

Purchases (Google Play and RevenueCat)

Dinner Bot Premium is sold through Google Play. Google processes your payment; we never see or hold your payment details. To know whether Premium is on for your device, the app uses RevenueCat, a purchase verification service. When purchases are enabled, RevenueCat receives:

  • An anonymous app user ID that its software generates for the app on your device. It is not your name, email, or Google account, and Dinner Bot has no accounts to link it to.
  • Your purchase and subscription status from Google Play: which product you bought, whether it is active, renewed, or cancelled, and the purchase token Google issues for it.
  • Basic technical information about the device needed to run the service, such as device type and operating system version.

RevenueCat processes this on our behalf to verify purchases and restore them on your devices. Its handling of that information is described in the RevenueCat privacy policy. The app saves only the resulting yes-or-no Premium status on your device, in a cache, so a store outage can never lock a paying user out of Premium.

In plain terms: your recipes and prices stay on your device; only purchase verification talks to the store.

Backups

The backup feature creates a file only when you tap it. The file contains your settings, your price book, your pantry and bulk stock, your own and imported recipes, your edits to library recipes, recipe ratings, barcode links, and scanned label facts. Meal plans and grocery lists are not in the file: importing a backup clears the device’s current plan and list, and you generate a fresh week from the restored data. Photos you attached to recipes are stored as separate files on the device and are not in the backup file either. You choose where the file goes (a drive, an email, another device) and who gets it. We never receive a copy. If you send a backup file somewhere that is not private, anyone with the file can read your household data, so treat it like any personal file.

Camera and notifications

The camera permission is used only for barcode scanning, only when you tap to scan. Camera frames are read on the device by Google’s ML Kit barcode library; frames are not stored and are not sent anywhere. Notifications, if you turn reminders on, are scheduled and shown entirely on the device. There is no push-notification server.

What we never do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting, or tracking software in the app.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email collection in the app.
  • We never sell personal information, and there is no household data of yours in our hands to sell.

Children

Dinner Bot is a household grocery and budgeting tool made for adults. It is not directed at children, and it collects no information from anyone, child or adult, beyond what this policy describes.

Your choices and deletion

  • Everything stored by the app can be removed by clearing the app’s storage in Android settings or by uninstalling the app.
  • Subscriptions are managed or cancelled in your Google Play account, under Payments & subscriptions. Cancelling stops renewal; it does not require deleting the app, and your data stays yours either way.
  • Purchase records held by RevenueCat are tied to the anonymous app user ID described above. If you want those records deleted, contact us at the address below and we will request deletion. Because the ID is anonymous, we may need the purchase details from your Google Play receipt to find the record.
  • Payment information is held by Google, not by us, under Google’s own privacy policy.
